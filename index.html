The Reality: Why are they endangered?

  • 🐻‍❄️ Polar Bears: Rapid loss of sea ice habitat due to climate change.
  • 🐅 Tigers: Illegal poaching for their fur and massive habitat destruction.
  • 🐢 Sea Turtles: Plastic pollution in oceans, getting caught in fishing nets, and hunting.
  • 🐋 Whales: Ship strikes, ocean noise pollution, and climate change affecting their food.
  • 🦛 Hippos: Habitat loss and illegal hunting for their meat and ivory teeth.

Writing: An Opinion Post

It's time to structure your thoughts. Read the two opinions below regarding zoos, and extract the connector phrases they use to organize their arguments.

For Zoos

Zoos do a lot of good work. First of all, they educate the public. This inspires the next generation of zookeepers, vets, and environmentalists. In addition, zoos help to protect endangered species through the breeding work they do. Last, zoos are involved in environmental programs of different kinds around the world. We need to support our zoos.

— Alassa, Lima, Peru

Against Zoos

Zoos are cruel and unnecessary. To start with, animals in captivity are bored and cannot behave naturally. Many animals find being on display very stressful. What's more, some animals die younger in zoos than they would in the wild. Last but not least, zoos care more about baby animals, which attract paying visitors. We should not support zoos.

— Jacob, New York, US

Language & Life: Problem Solving

Urban environments face significant challenges with stray animals. We will analyze how the UK solved this issue, and then brainstorm solutions for our own city.

The Trouble With Strays 🐕

Puppies are cute, aren't they? And kittens are adorable. But those puppies and kittens grow up to be adult dogs and cats. Often the demands of being a pet owner just get to be too much for people and the cats and dogs end up on the street as strays. Sometimes it's the responsibility of looking after an adult animal that causes problems, and sometimes there are financial reasons. It's a growing problem. In the city of Detroit in the US, it is estimated that there are 50,000 stray dogs. The World Health Organization estimates that there are about 200 million stray dogs worldwide.

This situation causes a number of problems. First of all, the lives of stray animals are hard and stressful. Every day is a struggle to find enough to eat and a safe place to sleep. That means the animals are often in bad health or even in danger of dying. In addition to that, stray animals cause a number of problems for city residents. They can run into the road and cause traffic accidents. Stray animals can also carry diseases. They can pass these diseases on to people or to domestic pets. Last, packs of dogs can be dangerous and may attack people.

However, there may be a solution to the problem. One country that has reduced the number of stray dogs is the UK. In 2016, a new law was introduced. All dogs must be microchipped. This means that a small chip—the size of a grain of rice—is inserted under the dog's skin. It's a quick and painless procedure. Microchips contain a unique code that is connected to a national database. When the chip is scanned, it shows the owner's address. If your dog does not have a chip, you can receive a fine of up to £500. Microchipping is useful if your dog is lost or stolen, and it also encourages people to take more responsibility for their pets. According to the Dogs Trust's Stray Dog Survey Report, in 2018, the number of stray animals in the UK was at its lowest level in 21 years—so this is a step in the right direction.
